Purpose and scope

What this document commits the product to, and the boundary of the first commercial release.

This BRD states the business requirements for a commercially licensed Fraud Risk Management System (FRMS) incorporating an Early Warning Signal (EWS) framework, built to the RBI Master Directions on Fraud Risk Management and the supervisory expectations that surround them. The platform was originally built against the three combined Directions issued 15 July 2024; RBI withdrew those on 31 July 2026 and replaced them with nine entity-specific Directions (RBI/DoS/2026-27/412–463) — see REG-01. The requirements below reflect the 2026 structure.

The platform is multi-tenant by design. One deployment serves many regulated entities, each with its own branding, its own board-approved thresholds, its own data and its own users. This is a deliberate commercial position: the compliance machinery an EWS framework requires is largely identical across institutions, while the thresholds and governance around it are emphatically not.

EWS coverage*

*Coverage of our own 33 indicators, which is a narrower claim than coverage of RBI's illustrative list — see the note below. 28 of 33 quantitative indicators are measurable once a tenant sends its feeds: 13 from the payment stream alone, CPT-02, CPT-03 and CPT-04 from screening lists, and BEH-02, BEH-03 and CBS-01 through CBS-10 from the core-banking event intake. The remaining five need records the platform cannot yet take — invoice lines, session geolocation and trade-finance documents — and are reported as unmeasurable with the reason, never scored as zero. See section 7.

On the RBI early-warning-signal list. The 2016 Master Directions on Frauds carried an illustrative annexure of 42 early-warning signals (widely reported as 45). The 2024 Master Directions on Fraud Risk Management (RBI/DOS/2024-25/118, 15 July 2024) supersede the 2016 Directions and enumerate no list at all — they require each bank's board-approved policy to define its own signals, attach EWS/RFA at the CRILC threshold of ₹3 crore, and examine an alert within 30 days. RBI withdrew the 2024 Directions in turn on 31 July 2026, splitting them into nine entity-specific instruments (RBI/DoS/2026-27/412–463) that share the same chapter backbone and the same no-enumerated-list, board-owns-its-own-signals design this note describes — see REG-01. The specific rupee and day figures above are stated as this platform's own configured defaults (see policy.py's threshold model per entity type) and must be confirmed against each entity type's own 2026 Direction text before being relied on as an exact regulatory figure.

Mapped against the 2016 annexure, the platform fully covers 40 of 42 signals, partially covers 2, and leaves 0 uncovered — every one of the 42 now maps to at least a partial indicator, and all but two map to a full one. Two catalogues share that coverage. Account-conduct signals that arrive as events — cheque returns, BG invocation and LC devolvement, interest funded by a fresh sanction, delayed repayment, diversion, cash withdrawal — are payment-rail and core-banking rules (CBS-01 through CBS-10 among them). A verified title-dispute register (CPT-04) and a verified related-party register (CBS-09/10) closed the last gaps in that catalogue this round, replacing the proxies (CPT-03's multiple-charge detection, CBS-01/03's group-exposure ratio) that previously stood in for them. The borrower-conduct and credit-monitoring signals — audited financials, stock-audit findings, ROC filings, auditor's reports — are read from a loan file on a periodic cycle, not from a payment stream in real time, by Lane C's own catalogue, LNC-01 through LNC-23. Several Lane C signals are built but unmeasurable until a tenant configures the matching reference feed, a credit officer records a finding, or a borrower's project-appraisal baseline and progress submission are both on file — reported as unmeasurable, never scored as zero. LNC-02 (project scope change, #3) also counts how many times a borrower's reported completion date has actually changed across every period on file, directly answering RBI's own word "frequent" rather than checking only the current period's own slip. LNC-11 (a negative rating action) has no counterpart on the list and is never counted toward the 42. The two remaining partial covers are both the same off-by-default local-LLM read of the annual report (#38, #39) — deliberately never promoted to "full": a probabilistic read should not claim to be deterministic. Conversely 20 of our 36 indicators have no counterpart on that list, because they are payment-fraud typologies answering a different question. The mapping is generated and kept current by docs/rbi_ews_mapping.py.

In scope

  • Transaction ingestion across UPI, IMPS, NEFT, RTGS and card rails
  • Inline decisioning (Lane A) — a go/no-go answer inside the payment window for rails a tenant configures for it, alongside the existing queue-drained detection lane. Shipped in shadow mode: every decision is computed, recorded and reportable, and the enforced answer stays allow until a tenant turns enforcement on. See BR-312 to BR-315
  • Rule-based EWS detection driven by each entity's configured catalogue
  • Alert triage, case management and the full RFA lifecycle including natural justice
  • Regulatory reporting to RBI (FMR) and FIU-IND (STR/CTR)
  • Board, supervisory, investigative and inspection-facing analytics
  • Tenant onboarding, branding, configuration and user administration

Explicitly out of scope for release 1

  • KYC/CKYC onboarding, AML customer risk rating and sanctions screening as primary systems — the platform consumes these as feeds
  • Cheque truncation, trade finance document examination and loan origination
  • Recovery, legal case management and LEA case tracking beyond referral records

Market and business case

Why a shared platform, and who buys it.

The July 2024 Directions extended a materially uniform fraud-governance obligation across three very different populations of regulated entity. A large commercial bank can fund a bespoke FRMS. A Tier-1 urban co-operative bank, a state co-operative bank or a mid-size NBFC carries the same obligations — board-approved policy, EWS framework, RFA classification, natural justice, FMR reporting — against a fraction of the technology budget. That asymmetry is the market.

Central product claim

Every figure the platform reports is derived from a single metric definition and is proven to reconcile at query time. A board pack, a supervisory return and an analyst's screen cannot disagree, because they are the same SQL. This is the differentiator against dashboards assembled from independent queries, and it is verifiable rather than asserted.

Threshold values are the entity's, never the vendor's

Response windows, filing deadlines, materiality limits and severity bands differ by entity type and tier, and each must be the figure that entity's own board approved. The platform ships documented defaults so a new tenant is never unconfigured, and treats every one of them as a value the tenant must review and own. No number in this document should be read as regulatory advice.

The outsourcing boundary

Vendor staff cannot unmask a bank's customer identifiers under any role. Operating the service does not require seeing the customers, and REG-06 makes this the first question an outsourcing review asks. It is enforced in the permission model and in the database grants, not by policy alone.

Phase 2b (write path, guardrails, versioning, capability) is built: a tenant with can_admin_tenant — checked dynamically, so a custom role granting it can manage roles too, not only the seeded tenant_admin — can create, edit and delete a role via POST/PUT/DELETE /tenants/{id}/roles. Guardrails refuse a tenant role reaching the platform-only administration module or the tenant_health dashboard, and refuse shadowing a reserved platform name; cross-tenant reach is structurally impossible since tenant_id is never client-supplied. Raising can_admin_tenant/can_reveal_pii is staged and needs a different eligible person to confirm (POST .../confirm, self_approval refused) — the same maker-checker asymmetry BR-715 applies elsewhere: lowering a privilege applies immediately, raising one does not. Every create/edit writes a full before/after snapshot to the audit trail rather than mutating history in place, so "what could this role do on date X" stays answerable without a separate version table. A custom role is assignable through BR-108's invite flow the moment it exists. A minimal console screen (list, create, delete, with the invite form's role picker now reading the tenant's real catalogue instead of a hardcoded list) ships with it.

Phase 2c (cross-service capability) is built: analytics-service, config-service, decision-service and notification-service now consult a tenant's custom roles too, via cp_common.dynamic_roles — a shared module every one of them imports, so the fetch-and-cache logic against tenant-service's new internal endpoint (GET /tenants/internal/{tenant_id}/roles) is written once, not five times. Every one of the ten fixed roles resolves with zero network calls, exactly as before — only a name outside that set triggers a lookup, cached against the same generation-token mechanism config-service's rule/policy changes already use, so a role edit is visible to every service the instant tenant-service bumps it, not after a clock interval. This covers the four generic capability primitives — module access, dashboard access, can_admin_tenant, can_reveal_pii — which is what every dashboard gate, every "or admin" fallback, and config-service's maker-checker eligibility across all four services actually run on. ingestion-service needed no change: it has no human role checks at all, only machine-credential scopes (BR-213). Verified against real HTTP round trips between services, not only in-process function calls, including a caught-and-fixed bug (the internal endpoint's URL was missing its router prefix) that a narrower test would have missed.

Phase 2d (risk_manager-equivalent capability) is built: TenantRole gained a third flag, can_activate_config — may propose or confirm a configuration activation (BR-715), independent of can_admin_tenant. config-service's maker_checker.ensure_eligible() now checks this one flag dynamically instead of a hardcoded name list; tenant_admin and platform_admin carry it alongside full admin scope, risk_manager carries it on its own — exactly the distinction its own name always implied but the code never expressed as a settable grant. A custom role can now be given that narrower capability without also becoming a tenant administrator, or vice versa: administering a tenant (users, branding, other roles) no longer implies the power to activate fraud-detection configuration, and the two are verified independent of each other. Raising the flag is staged through the same maker-checker elevation as the other two — a different eligible person must confirm — and it is visible in the console's role table and create-role form alongside Admin and PII. A tenant-defined role is otherwise fully functional platform-wide: created via BR-113's write path, assignable to a user via BR-108, and recognised for module/dashboard access and all three capability flags in every service that gates on them. What remains open is narrower still: a small number of call sites gate an action behind a named list mixing in roles no flag on this platform describes at all (analytics-service's FILING_ROLES/CTR_ROLES, which include principal_officer) — extending those needs further capability flags, real and separately-scoped, not attempted here		SHOULDBuilt

Assumptions, constraints and exclusions

Assumptions

  • Each tenant can expose transaction data by API or file within minutes of settlement
  • Each tenant's board approves its own thresholds; the vendor supplies defaults, never advice
  • Banks will federate identity from an existing IdP rather than manage users in the platform long-term
  • Reference data (sanctions, CERSAI, CBS extracts) can be licensed or supplied by the tenant

Constraints

  • Payment data must remain in India, constraining hosting and any sub-processor
  • Some tenants require air-gapped or private-cloud deployment; no runtime dependency on an external CDN
  • Money is integer paise throughout; no floating-point representation is permitted anywhere in the stack
  • Reported figures must reconcile exactly, which rules out eventually-consistent read models for reported metrics

Exclusions

  • The platform is not a system of record for customers, accounts or the general ledger
  • It does not authorise, block or reverse payments
  • It does not provide legal advice on classification; it enforces the process around the decision
